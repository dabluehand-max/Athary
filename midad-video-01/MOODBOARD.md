# MOODBOARD: مِداد, video 01

**Research limit:** YouTube, Vimeo and museum sites are blocked by this environment's network policy, so no reference videos were downloaded or frame-analysed yet. The directions below come from the Midad posts in the "بوستات" folder and from well-known historical references (Hannah Höch's Dada photomontages, Terry Gilliam's cut-out animation, Matisse's late cut-outs *as a process*, not as imagery). Once the network opens, 5–8 references get pulled with yt-dlp and this file gets reference frames.

## A. Ink on newsprint (chosen)
- **Palette:** off-white paper `#EEE9DE`, black paper `#171615`, B&W photos with warm lifted blacks, one accent: ballpoint ink blue `#1F3FA8`.
- **Texture:** fibre paper, newsprint, masking tape, white scissor-cut borders around every photo.
- **Motion:** on 12 fps steps inside 30 fps; pieces land with a shadow that shrinks as they settle; ±1.5 px / ±0.4° wobble on held frames; a real hand places and cuts.
- **Type:** Lalezar and Rakkas for cut letters, Amiri for print (newspaper, dates, names), IBM Plex Sans Arabic for typed strips and captions.
- **Refs:** Höch (sharp scissor edges, scale clashes), Gilliam (flat pieces, snappy movement, hard cuts).

## B. Sadu textile (brand-led)
- Cream, navy, Sadu reds, mustard sun, red thread linking figures.
- Fabric scans instead of paper, stitched edges, thread drawn between people.
- Slower rhythm: pieces "sewn" on.
- Closest to the current posts, but reads as heritage and weakens the Matisse/ink story.

## C. Blue-pigment body (raw)
- Your workshop footage of hands smearing blue pigment as the base layer; cut-outs float over wet colour.
- Faster, more aggressive cuts; the body-map canvas as the hero.
- Strong but splits focus between two textures (wet paint vs. paper).

## Pick: A
1. مِداد means ink, and blue ballpoint is your signature, so one ink-blue accent carries the name without explanation.
2. B&W newsprint makes photos of very different quality (phone shots, studio portraits) look like one family.
3. Scene 2 needs full colour for the Matisse-style shapes; a mostly monochrome base lets that moment land.
